How We Score Fishing Times (and Do Solunar Tables Work?)

Every 10 minutes gets a score for each fish. The good factors are added up and squeezed, so they can't run away: score = 3 + 7 × (1 − e−points/4), then the bad factors are taken off. An ordinary dawn or dusk scores about 6, dusk plus a solunar period about 7, and a 9 needs almost everything to line up. A 10 never happens. The best hour of a day is the best 60 minutes in a row.

Which fish leads: each lake has a signature fish (the best TPWD rating there, then the most popular in Texas). The headline shows it unless another fish scores at least 1.5 points more. Fish in a closed season never lead.

What we never do

We never recommend a fish during a closed season (southern flounder can't be kept from Nov 1 to Dec 14). The headline hour needs a sourced reason (the fish's feeding light or moving tide). If no fish has one in the next 24 hours, for example where the tide is tiny, we still show the best hour but mark it as a slow day. We never score a day the NWS hasn't forecast yet. We never invent a fishing rule: if we could not find a source, that factor stays neutral and the page says so. We never promise a catch.

Do solunar tables work?

Maybe a little. The theory is old and popular, but we have not found strong scientific evidence that it works. That is why solunar periods add at most 1.75 points here, less than dawn and dusk light (2.5), and they can never put a fish in the headline on their own. If you only look at a solunar table, you are using the weakest part of the forecast.
